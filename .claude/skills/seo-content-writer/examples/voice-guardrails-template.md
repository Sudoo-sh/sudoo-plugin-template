# Voice Guardrails Template

Copy this template, fill it in once using your writing samples (see Phase 1.1 in SKILL.md), and paste it into every writing prompt going forward.

---

```
VOICE GUARDRAILS

TONE:
- [Add 5–8 adjectives: e.g. direct, practical, conversational, slightly dry, no-fluff]

SENTENCE STRUCTURE:
- [e.g. Mix of short punchy sentences and longer analytical ones]
- [e.g. Average paragraph: 2–4 sentences]
- [e.g. Avoid nested clauses]

VOCABULARY:
- [e.g. Plain English over jargon — explain technical terms on first use]
- [e.g. Use industry language but don't perform expertise]

DO:
- Start with a concrete situation, not an abstraction
- Use specific numbers and examples over vague claims
- Acknowledge trade-offs — don't pretend everything is simple
- Write to one reader, not a crowd

DON'T:
- Use: "just", "delve", "enhance", "leverage", "game-changing", "innovative", "seamless", "holistic", "robust", "unlock"
- Use em-dashes as a crutch — replace with period, comma, or colon
- Use "Bold term: description" list format — very AI-looking
- Use passive voice where active is possible

DO NOT START WITH:
- Proverbs, sayings, or "everyone knows that…"
- Generalizations: "every marketer has…", "all founders…"
- Direct questions to the reader: "Have you ever noticed…"
- Quotes from classics or famous people
- "In today's [adjective] world…"

VOICE EXAMPLES:
Instead of: "Leveraging AI tools can enhance your content strategy."
Write: "AI tools can cut your research time in half — if you know where to apply them."

Instead of: "It's important to consider your audience."
Write: "Before you write a word, ask: what is this specific reader trying to figure out?"
```

## How to Generate Your Own Voice Guardrails

Use this prompt in Claude with 3–5 of your best writing samples:

```
Analyze these writing samples and create my Voice DNA:

1. Tone descriptors (8–10 specific adjectives)
2. Sentence structure patterns (average length, variety, complexity)
3. Vocabulary level and word choices
4. Paragraph rhythm and flow
5. Common transitional phrases I use
6. How I handle examples and analogies
7. My approach to hooks and conclusions
8. Phrases or patterns I repeat (identify habits)

Then create a VOICE GUARDRAILS block I can paste into every future Claude prompt.
Include specific DO / DON'T examples based on my actual writing — not generic advice.

[Paste your 3–5 articles here]
```

## Session Warm-Up Prompt

Paste this at the start of any writing session where you need Claude to match your voice:

```
Before writing anything, review my voice guardrails below.
After producing each output, self-check:
1. Does this sound like me?
2. Are sentence lengths varied in a way that matches my style?
3. Have I avoided everything on my DON'T list?
If not, revise before showing me the output.

[PASTE YOUR VOICE GUARDRAILS HERE]
```
